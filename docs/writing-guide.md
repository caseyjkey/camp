# Key Camp writing guide

Write for a young person who is learning a concept for the first time. Keep
Casey's voice: direct, curious, and respectful. These guidelines draw on the
clarity goals of ASD-STE100 and Strunk & White; they do not establish formal
ASD-STE100 compliance. Formal compliance requires the current standard, its
approved vocabulary and rules, and a separate review.

## Lessons

- Give each lesson one clear mission. Explain why the task matters in plain words.
- Start each numbered step with an action verb. Put steps in the order students
  will perform them. Split a step when it asks for separate actions.
- Prefer short sentences and active voice. Address the student as “you.”
- Explain a new technical term in context before relying on it. Keep correct
  technical names, commands, and safety warnings; do not simplify away meaning.
- Describe a visible result for each success criterion. Distinguish what a
  student can check from a claim that still needs evidence.
- Say what to do when something fails. Do not guess at the cause or promise
  a result that depends on the network, another person, or an outside service.
- Preserve accessibility, privacy, and supervision instructions. Do not trade
  safety for brevity.
- Avoid filler, slogans, unsupported claims, and unnecessary em dashes. Read
  the result aloud; short writing should still sound human.

## Review before publishing

1. Can a first-time student understand the mission and complete the steps in order?
2. Are all unfamiliar terms explained or linked to a trusted explanation?
3. Can a student check the result without trusting the agent's claim alone?
4. Are permissions, private information, and recovery steps clear where relevant?
5. Has an instructor checked technical accuracy and age-appropriate language?
